# Nobody Is Looking

**Does published AI-safety research reproduce?** 741 posts read and measured; 36 of their
experiments re-run on one second-hand graphics card; 17 further runs asking what a reproduction
actually *depends* on.

---

# Human

Two years of empirical AI-safety research lives on the Alignment Forum and LessWrong: 741 posts —
728 once duplicates and link-posts are removed — written by 893 people, most of it never
peer-reviewed and nearly all of it never re-run by anyone. This project read all of it, then tried
to re-run as much of it as a single RTX 3090 could hold.

The first thing you notice is who is doing the work. Five hundred people first-authored something
here; **370 of them did it exactly once** and were never seen again. Above them sits a thin
supervisory layer — Neel Nanda is an author on 52 posts and first author on none of them. And
scattered through the tail are a handful of people doing something nobody asked them to: `chanind`
published eleven papers on sparse autoencoders in eighteen months, four of them arguing that
sparse autoencoders don't work as advertised. Håvard Tveit Ihle published the same benchmark five
times over nineteen months, which in a corpus where three-quarters of authors appear once makes him
almost the only person measuring anything twice.

That matters because of what the corpus does *not* contain. Of 728 substantive posts, 24 are
replications — 3.3%. Twenty-three different people wrote them; exactly one person did it twice.
Meanwhile 64% of posts position themselves against prior work. The field generates disagreement
roughly twenty times faster than it resolves it, and you can see where the disagreements pile up:
of the fifty best-studied phenomena, thirty-five carry both positive and negative findings. When we
looked at how those thirty-five were measured, 493 posts named **493 distinct metrics**, and not one
metric was shared between a post saying yes and a post saying no. "Contested" here doesn't mean
people measured the same thing and disagreed. It means nobody measured the same thing twice.

So we ran them. Thirty-six experiments, pinned to the authors' own commits, fixing the environment
and nothing else, with a pass/fail rule written down before each run.

**Sixteen never produced a number at all** — and not one of them failed on the science. They failed
because a library moved, or a file the author used was never committed, or the code assumed a
machine we don't have. So we went back to eight of them and rebuilt the environment properly:
frozen to the package versions available on the day the post was published, with missing files
regenerated by the authors' own scripts. Seven then ran. Three matched their papers exactly, four
matched in part, none contradicted its author. In none of the eight did the first error message name
the real problem — underneath were uncommitted files, a lockfile that could never have resolved, code
that was broken the day it was published, and, in one case, a bug in our own harness that we had
filed against someone else's repository.

Of everything that reached a measurement, **25 of 27 reproduced at least partially. One was a
genuine scientific miss** — and even that one is more interesting than a failure: AntiPaSTO's
honesty-steering result reproduces on the small model and not the headline one, because the config
the repository ships was never the config the paper used. `git log -S` settles it in twenty seconds.

The uncomfortable finding came last. Having established that the code mostly works, we started
changing one thing at a time. Library versions turned out not to matter: swapping `transformers`
left all 35,700 generated answers byte-for-byte identical, and a major PyTorch jump rewrote
two-thirds of the model's text while changing no conclusion at all. **Then we changed the random
seed.** One reproduced result turned out to land inside its own published range on four seeds out of
fifteen. Another — a published trick for catching a model that hides its abilities — works on two
draws in three and *destroys the model* on one in four; the original author's "6 of 10" and our
"9 of 10" were both ordinary draws from the same coin. The first ten seeds we ran were the least
representative ten of the thirty.

The field reports a seed count in **13.7%** of posts. It states a comparison condition in 90.7% and
lists its own limitations in 89.1%. The form of an empirical report has converged completely; the
statistics underneath it have not. No post in 728 satisfies all six basic design markers.

One last pattern, because it changes what you should do with this repository. Sort the posts by
karma and the share that ships runnable code falls from 75% in the bottom decile to 47% in the top.
The mechanism isn't laziness — it's that the most-read questions are about frontier models, which
live behind an API. **The work everyone reads is the work nobody can check**, and ranking
replication targets by attention selects against the ones you can actually run.

Nobody is looking. It turns out that when you do look, most of it holds up — and the things that
don't are almost never the things anyone was worried about.

# LLM

## Object

741 posts (union of an Alignment Forum scrape n=253 and a LessWrong scrape n=637, overlap 149),
window 2024-08-25 → 2026-08-25. Two questions: what does this literature claim, and does it
reproduce on one RTX 3090 (24 GB).

## Evidence tiers, used throughout

`[MEASURED]` a script in this repo emits it · `[INFERRED]` a judgment resting on measured numbers ·
`[SPECULATIVE]` a hypothesis · `[UNRESOLVED]` the data cannot decide. Every rate carries an *n* and
a Wilson interval; every median a bootstrap interval.

## Findings

- `[MEASURED]` **Reproduction, N=36:** 20 rows reached a measurement, 15 reproduced (11 exact +
  4 recompute), 18/20 ≥ partial. 16 never ran — all on packaging or hardware, none on the claim.
- `[MEASURED]` **Rot is reversible.** 8 never-ran rows re-attempted with the environment
  reconstructed: 7 reached a measurement → 3 exact + 4 partial, 0 scientific misses; the 8th needs a
  login. Pooled: **25/27 ≥ partial**. Exceptions: AntiPaSTO (the one miss) and one hosted-API row
  that was never a local test.
- `[MEASURED]` **`env` is five distinct classes** — post-date version drift, uncommitted artefacts
  (most common), code or lockfiles broken at publication, harness bugs, credential gates. The first
  traceback named the terminal cause in 0 of 6 rows.
- `[MEASURED]` **Seeds move verdicts; library versions do not.** One reproduced row is bimodal
  across seeds (4/15 inside the claimed range). A "for some seeds" claim is 67% [49, 81] with a
  23% [12, 41] model-collapse rate at n=30. `transformers` minor change → 35,700/35,700 generations
  byte-identical; torch major → 65% of text rewritten, no verdict changed (seed SD / kernel SD = 3.5).
- `[MEASURED]` **Blind bug-injection control: 7/7.** Clean and bug-injected reruns of three
  reproduced rows, judged blind — every injected bug caught, every clean arm confirmed.
- `[MEASURED]` **Corpus:** replications 3.3% [2.2, 4.9] vs 64.1% positioning against prior work;
  35 of the 50 best-studied phenomena contested; 493/493 distinct metrics across the 29 contested
  phenomena with n ≥ 6, 0 shared across sign; baseline 90.7%, limitations 89.1%, uncertainty 19.4%,
  seeds 13.7%, prereg 2.2%; no post scores 6/6 on design markers.
- `[MEASURED]` **Attention ↔ reproducibility:** code release falls 75% → 47% across karma deciles,
  mediated by model access (closed-model posts 66% → 31%; the gradient vanishes among open-model
  posts). `[INFERRED]` Ranking replication candidates by attention selects against runnability.

## Constraints on any continuation

1. Replication rows take environment-only fixes, each logged. Changing the model, seed set, library
   epoch, dataset size or metric makes it an **extension** (`experiment_class: "extension"`), which
   never enters the reproduction rate.
2. No hosted-model APIs (→ `api-key`), no accounts or logins (→ `model-access`).
3. No pushes to authors' repositories, no issues, no author contact, no new forks.
4. A 3-decimal match obtained with the author's own seed is `exact-same-seed`, not a robust effect.
   A seeded result's honest tier is a rate with an interval.
5. Do not read the 2026H2 period as a trend — 56 days against 181–184.
6. The corpus-level rigor rates come from an LLM judge no human has validated (`prompts.md` P4 is
   the design). Contrasts are more robust than levels.

## Caveats that travel with the headline

The replication sample is minute-class rows plus three protocol experiments; the hours-class test is
n=2, one of which used the recompute path. Consistent at scale, not confirmed. One machine, one
operator: four failures are this card's limits, not universal.

## Not in this repository

`union.json` and `p3/posts/` — the verbatim text of 741 posts by ~900 authors. That is the scrapes'
to publish, not this repo's; `build_union.py` rebuilds them. Cloned upstream repositories, virtual
environments, model weights, activation dumps and progress-bar logs are excluded too; every
exclusion is explained in [`.gitignore`](.gitignore).
